# Numerical Relations and Reasoning

> Seating and ordering puzzles, age and work problems, clocks, and speed–distance–time questions.

## Strategies for puzzles

1. Draw a diagram (a row or circle of slots, or a table).
2. Place **fixed** facts first (ends, exact positions), then relative ones ("immediately right of").
3. Branch on the remaining possibilities; eliminate the branches that contradict a clue.
4. "Immediately right" means adjacent; "to the right" means anywhere to the right.

## Key formulas

| Topic | Formula |
|---|---|
| Speed | distance = speed × time; 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s |
| Train passing a pole | time = train length / speed |
| Train passing a platform | time = (train + platform length) / speed |
| Relative speed | opposite directions: add; same direction: subtract |
| Work | rate = 1/time; together $\frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{A} + \frac{1}{B}$ |
| Clock | minute hand 6°/min; hour hand 0.5°/min; angle $= |30H - 5.5M|$ |
| Ages | set up linear equations with a common unknown |

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** C is at the left end, E at the right end, D immediately right of C, and B immediately right of A. Order: C D A B E, so A is in the middle.

**Example 2.** A 200 m train at 72 km/h (= 20 m/s) passes a pole in 10 s.

**Example 3.** A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days: together $\frac1{10} + \frac1{15} = \frac16$ ⇒ 6 days.

**Example 4.** Clock at 3:15: $|30\times3 - 5.5\times15| = |90 - 82.5| = 7.5°$.

**Example 5.** A is 3 years older than B, who is 3 years older than C; C is 10, so A is 16.

## Common traps

- Forgetting to convert km/h to m/s.
- Ignoring the hour hand's movement in clock problems.

## Summary

- Fixed clues first, then relative ones; draw the arrangement.
- Speed conversion ×5/18; combined work rates add; clock angle $|30H - 5.5M|$.
