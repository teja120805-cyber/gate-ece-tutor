# Data Graphs, Tables and Charts

> Reading bar charts, pie charts, line graphs and tables, and computing percentages, ratios, averages and growth, appear in every GA section.

## Intuition

Data-interpretation questions are arithmetic dressed up as charts. Read the axes, units and totals carefully; most errors come from misreading the data rather than from the calculation.

## Key formulas

- **Percentage change** $= \dfrac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}}\times100\%$.
- **Percentage share** (pie chart) $= \dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}\times100\%$; a sector angle $= \text{share}\times360°$.
- **Average** $= \dfrac{\text{sum}}{\text{count}}$; a weighted average uses the weights.
- **Ratio** $a:b$; to compare growth, use ratios rather than absolute differences.
- **CAGR** $= \left(\dfrac{\text{final}}{\text{initial}}\right)^{1/n} - 1$.

## Strategies

1. Note the units (thousands, lakhs, crores, %) and whether the values are cumulative.
2. **Approximate** when the options are far apart; compute exactly when they are close.
3. For "which year had the highest growth?", compare the **percentage** change, not the absolute change.
4. Pie charts: if the total is given, convert percentages to values; a 90° sector is 25%.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Revenue rose from 200 to 250: growth $= 50/200 = 25\%$.

**Example 2.** A budget of 1200 with 30% for education: $0.3\times1200 = 360$.

**Example 3.** Quarterly sales 40, 60, 50, 70: average $= 220/4 = 55$; the ratio of Q4 to Q1 is $70/40 = 1.75$.

**Example 4.** Units sold 120 → 150 → 180: the increase from year 1 to year 3 is $60/120 = 50\%$ (not $25\% + 20\%$).

## Common traps

- Adding successive percentage changes.
- Using the wrong base year for a percentage change.

## Summary

- Percentage change uses the old value as the base; pie share × 360° gives the angle.
- Read units and totals carefully; compare growth in percentages.
