# Basic English Grammar

> General Aptitude includes 1-mark grammar questions on tenses, articles, prepositions, subject–verb agreement and conditionals.

## Intuition

Most grammar questions test a handful of rules. Read the whole sentence, find the subject and the time frame, then check each option against the rule.

## Key rules

**Subject–verb agreement**
- *Each, every, either, neither, everyone, anybody* take singular verbs: "Each of the students **has**…".
- *Neither A nor B* / *either A or B*: the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.
- Collective nouns (team, committee) are usually singular in Indian/British exam usage when acting as a unit.
- Phrases such as *along with* or *as well as* do not change the subject's number: "The manager, along with his team, **is**…".

**Tenses**
- Present perfect for actions that started in the past and continue: "has worked here **since** 2019 / **for** five years".
- Use *since* with a point in time and *for* with a duration.
- Past perfect for the earlier of two past actions: "The train **had left** when we arrived."

**Articles**
- *a/an* follow the **sound**: an hour, an honest man, a university, a one-way street.
- *the* for specific or unique things (the Sun, the Ganga).

**Conditionals**
- Unreal present: "If I **were** you, I **would**…".
- Unreal past: "If he **had** studied, he **would have** passed."

**Prepositions** (common pairs): *good at*, *interested in*, *depend on*, *capable of*, *prefer X to Y*, *different from*.

**Adjectives:** comparatives for two items (*better*), superlatives for three or more (*best*); never double them ("more better" is wrong).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** "Neither the students nor the teacher **was** present." (The teacher is nearer and singular.)

**Example 2.** "She is **an** honest engineer" (silent h).

**Example 3.** "If I **were** you, I would accept the offer."

## Common traps

- Agreeing the verb with the nearest noun inside a prepositional phrase ("The quality of the products **is**…", not "are").
- Using *since* with a duration.

## Summary

- Each/every ⇒ singular; neither…nor ⇒ the nearer subject.
- *Since* + point in time, *for* + duration; articles follow sound; unreal conditionals use *were* / *had*.
