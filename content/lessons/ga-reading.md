# Reading and Comprehension

> GA includes short passages with questions on the main idea, inference, assumptions and the author's tone.

## Intuition

GATE passages are short. The questions test whether you can separate what the passage **states**, what it **implies**, and what it merely **mentions**. The correct answer is supported by the text — neither more extreme nor beyond its scope.

## Question types and strategies

| Type | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Main idea | the central claim, often in the first or last sentence; reject options that are too narrow (one detail) or too broad |
| Inference | something that **must** be true given the passage, not just possible |
| Assumption | an unstated premise the argument depends on (negation test: if negating it breaks the argument, it is the assumption) |
| Tone | the author's attitude, revealed by word choice (e.g. "well-intentioned but poorly executed" is critical yet balanced) |
| Strengthen / weaken | new information that supports or undermines the link between evidence and conclusion |

## Traps in the options

- **Extreme language** ("always", "never", "all") is usually wrong unless the passage says so.
- **Out of scope:** statements that may be true in the real world but are not supported by the passage.
- **Reversed causality** or a distorted detail.

## Worked example

*Passage:* "Although solar panels have become much cheaper, rural adoption remains slow because many households cannot afford the upfront cost, even when long-term savings are substantial."

- **Main reason** for slow adoption: the upfront cost.
- **Valid inference:** schemes that spread the cost over time could increase adoption.
- **Not inferable:** that rural households are unaware of solar energy (never stated).

## Summary

- Answer from the passage, not outside knowledge.
- Inference = must be true; assumption = a necessary unstated premise; beware extreme and out-of-scope options.
