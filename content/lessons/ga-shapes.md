# Transformation of Shapes

> Spatial-aptitude questions on translation, rotation, scaling and mirroring of figures and coordinates.

## Intuition

Spatial questions get much easier when you track a few reference points — a corner, a coordinate, an asymmetric feature — instead of the whole picture. Apply the transformation to those points and rebuild the figure.

## Transformations in coordinates

| Transformation | Rule for a point $(x, y)$ |
|---|---|
| translation by $(a, b)$ | $(x + a, y + b)$ |
| rotation 90° counter-clockwise about the origin | $(-y, x)$ |
| rotation 90° clockwise | $(y, -x)$ |
| rotation 180° | $(-x, -y)$ |
| reflection in the x-axis | $(x, -y)$ |
| reflection in the y-axis | $(-x, y)$ |
| reflection in the line $y = x$ | $(y, x)$ |
| scaling by $k$ about the origin | $(kx, ky)$ — lengths ×$k$, area ×$k^2$ |

- Rotations and reflections preserve size and shape. A reflection reverses orientation (handedness); a rotation does not.

## Mirror and symmetry facts

- In a **vertical** mirror, left and right swap: b ↔ d, p ↔ q. Letters with a vertical axis of symmetry (A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y) look unchanged.
- In a **horizontal** mirror (water image), top and bottom swap: b ↔ p, d ↔ q. Letters with a horizontal axis (B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X) look unchanged.
- A clock's mirror time is $11{:}60$ minus the actual time (for a vertical mirror).

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** $(2, 3)$ rotated 90° counter-clockwise: $(-3, 2)$.

**Example 2.** $(4, -1)$ reflected in the y-axis: $(-4, -1)$.

**Example 3.** A square of side 2 scaled by 3 has area $36$ (= 4 × 9).

**Example 4.** The minute hand turns 6° per minute, so 210° takes 35 minutes.

## Summary

- Learn the coordinate rules for rotation and reflection; scaling multiplies area by $k^2$.
- A vertical mirror swaps left and right; a horizontal mirror swaps top and bottom.
