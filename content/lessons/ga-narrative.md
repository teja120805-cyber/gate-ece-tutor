# Narrative Sequencing

> Arrange jumbled sentences into a coherent paragraph — a regular GA question type.

## Intuition

Good paragraphs have a logical skeleton: an opening that introduces the topic, a development in time or cause order, and a conclusion. Clues inside the sentences — pronouns, connectives, and the move from general to specific — reveal which sentence must follow which.

## Strategies

1. **Find the opener:** a sentence that introduces the subject and does not depend on anything earlier (no "this", "however" or "he" referring back).
2. **Find mandatory pairs:**
   - a pronoun or "this/these" must follow the sentence that introduces its noun;
   - *however* follows a statement it contrasts with;
   - *as a result / therefore* follows a cause;
   - *first … then … finally* signals sequence.
3. **Use chronology and process order:** events or steps in time order (collect data → analyse → conclude).
4. **Find the closer:** a conclusion, result or summary.
5. **Use the options:** once you fix one pair or the opener, eliminate the options that violate it.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.**
- P: He boarded the train.
- Q: He bought a ticket.
- R: He reached the station.
- S: The train left.

Chronological order: R → Q → P → S.

**Example 2.**
- P: However, the costs were higher than expected.
- Q: The project began with great enthusiasm.
- R: As a result, funding was cut.
- S: Initial progress was rapid.

Q (opener) → S (development) → P ("however" contrasts with the progress) → R (result). Order: Q S P R.

## Summary

- Opener = an independent topic sentence; pronouns and connectives create forced pairs.
- Use chronology and cause → effect; eliminate options quickly.
